1. Introduction to Jazz Harmony

Jazz harmony involves the construction and interaction of chords and scales. It diverges from [traditional classical harmony](music-theory.html) by incorporating extended and altered chords, which adds richness and depth to jazz music.

Seventh Chords

Seventh chords are fundamental to jazz harmony, and they are created by adding a seventh note to the basic triad. Let's explore some common seventh chords used in jazz:

  • Major 7th: A major triad + major 7th (C - E - G - B)
  • Dominant 7th: A major triad + minor 7th (C - E - G - B♭)
  • Minor 7th: A minor triad + minor 7th (C - E♭ - G - B♭)
  • Half-Diminished 7th: A diminished triad + minor 7th (C - E♭ - G♭ - B♭)
  • Diminished 7th: A diminished triad + diminished 7th (C - E♭ - G♭ - B♭♭)

C7 (Dominant 7th): C - E - G - B♭

2. Jazz Scales

Jazz music incorporates various scales that differ from classical scales. Let’s explore some of the most widely used jazz scales.

Major Scale

C Major Scale: C - D - E - F - G - A - B - C

Minor Scales

There are three commonly used minor scales in jazz:

  • Natural Minor: C - D - E♭ - F - G - A♭ - B♭ - C
  • Harmonic Minor: C - D - E♭ - F - G - A♭ - B - C
  • Melodic Minor (Ascending): C - D - E♭ - F - G - A - B - C

Blues Scale

C Blues Scale: C - E♭ - F - F♯ - G - B♭ - C

Pentatonic Scale

C Pentatonic Scale: C - D - E - G - A - C

3. Jazz Chord Progressions

Jazz progressions are based on chords that move in specific patterns to create a sense of harmonic movement. Let’s dive into some common progressions.

ii - V - I Progression

This is the most common jazz progression, often used in a variety of jazz standards. It involves a ii minor chord, a V dominant chord, and resolving to the I major chord:

ii - V - I in C Major: Dm7 - G7 - Cmaj7

Turnaround Progression

The turnaround is a brief progression that "turns" the music back to the beginning. A common 4-bar turnaround is:

Turnaround: Cmaj7 - A7 - Dm7 - G7

4. Rhythm and Syncopation in Jazz

Jazz rhythm is distinctive for its use of syncopation, where offbeats are emphasized, creating a unique "swing" feel.

How to Play Swing Rhythm:

  1. Play two eighth notes, but instead of playing them evenly, emphasize the first note by giving it slightly more time than the second.
  2. Practice with a metronome set to triplet feel to develop the proper timing.
  3. Once comfortable, try improvising with this rhythmic style over chord progressions.

5. Improvisation in Jazz

Improvisation is central to jazz. It involves creating melodies spontaneously over a given chord progression. Here are some key improvisation tips:

Improvisation Practice:

  1. Start with a simple ii - V - I progression in C major.
  2. Use only chord tones (C, E, G, B for Cmaj7) to start your improvisation.
  3. Gradually add passing tones and blues notes (C - E♭ - G - B♭ for C7) to add color.
  4. Practice focusing on rhythm and expression while improvising.

6. Advanced Jazz Concepts

As you become more familiar with jazz, you can dive into more complex harmonic and melodic ideas such as modal jazz, chord substitutions, and chromaticism.

Modal Jazz

C Dorian Mode: C - D - E♭ - F - G - A - B♭ - C

Chromaticism

Chromaticism refers to using notes that are outside the scale or key to add tension and color.
